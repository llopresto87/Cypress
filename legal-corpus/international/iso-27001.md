## COPYRIGHT — the rule that governs this whole page

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and ISO/IEC 27002:2022 are copyrighted works sold by ISO/IEC
and the national standards bodies. **Nothing on this page reproduces, quotes, or
paraphrases-as-if-quoted any normative clause or Annex A control text.**

Every entry here therefore carries
`text_form: wording withheld — requires licensed copy` or `topic only`, and:

- **A control is citable by identifier and published title. It is not citable
  by wording.** "A.8.10 — Information Deletion" is a legitimate citation;
  anything purporting to state *what A.8.10 requires* is not, and must be read
  out of a licensed copy.
- Control **identifiers and short titles** are reproduced because they circulate
  openly across ISO's own free preview pages, certification bodies and GRC
  vendors as short-title references. The **requirement, purpose and guidance
  text** does not, and is not here.
- `licensed_copy_required_for` on each entry says exactly what a reader cannot
  get from this corpus.

This is a correctness requirement, not a formatting preference. Reproducing the
normative text would infringe; paraphrasing it as if quoted would fabricate.

**This page is therefore deliberately thin on substantive text, and that is the
correct shape for a `standard` entry — not a defect and not a gap to be filled.**
A reader who wants to know what a control *requires* is meant to leave here and
open a licensed copy. Nothing on this page should ever grow to compensate for
the withheld wording; a page that reads as though it tells you what ISO requires
would be a worse artifact than a thin one, because it would be wrong in a way
that looks right.

## Standing fields — the inheritable half of the entry contract

Direct fetches of `iso.org` — catalogue and OBP preview pages alike — returned
**HTTP 403** to the ingest tooling. **No entry on this page is
`primary-fetched`, and none can be until that changes.** That one sourcing limit
shapes every inheritable field, so they are stated once here instead of being
restated under each entry (`../_schema.md`, "The citability contract"):

- **Stated per entry, never inherited:** `instrument` — this page holds five
  distinct standards plus one scheme-practice entry and one verified absence —
  together with `provision`, `official_url` (each entry names its own ISO
  catalogue or OBP record), and the `standard` kind's own `title_source` and
  `licensed_copy_required_for`.
- **consulted — two grades, split per entry and never averaged to the page:**
  - **`not-fetched`** — `iso-27001-2022`, `iso-27002-2022`, `iso-27701-2019`,
    `iso-27001-2013`: ISO catalogue search-result metadata (for the 2013
    edition, references inside secondary sources), cross-checked against
    Wikipedia. **Nothing was read end-to-end.**
  - **`secondary-corroborated`** — every other entry, including the Clause 4–10
    table, `iso-27001-annex-a-2022` and every `iso-27001-a-…` control id: named
    secondary sources (Wikipedia, isms.online, forensicspot.com, GRC vendors)
    that *were* fetched and found mutually consistent. **That consistency raises
    confidence; it does not make any of it primary.**

  Each entry names the sources it actually rests on and restates its own
  `verification_grade`, because the grade must be legible at the point of use.
- **language_version:** English throughout. The **edition** is part of each
  standard's identity and therefore stays per entry — 27001:2022 third edition,
  Amd 1:2024, 27002:2022, 27701:2019, and the withdrawn 27001:2013 second
  edition.
- **verified:** 2026-07-31, for every entry on this page.
- **legal_status:** `published — current edition` — with **three deliberate
  exceptions, which must never be overwritten by this default**:
  `iso-27001-2013` is **`withdrawn`** (superseded by `iso-27001-2022`, kept for
  lineage); `iso-27001-certification-cycle` is **`n/a`** because it records
  accreditation-scheme practice rather than normative text; and
  `iso-gdpr-art-42-relationship` is
  **`not recorded — no such EDPB statement found`**, a verified absence and
  citable as one.

Where an entry states any of these fields inline, **the entry's own value wins.**

### 3.5 Two standing cautions about control titles

Both are facts about the *standard*, not about any reader of it, and both exist
because a short title invites an over-reading:

- **A.8.10 "Information Deletion" is not a synonym for a statutory erasure
  right.** An ISO control's scope and a data-protection statute's legal-basis
  scope are not identical, and a title that sounds like a statutory concept is
  not a mapping to it.
- **A.5.34 "Privacy and Protection of PII" is the one Annex A control explicitly
  named for PII at the organizational level** — which makes it the most direct
  Annex A anchor for privacy-adjacent work, and **not a substitute for the
  statutory analysis itself.**

Selecting which controls apply, and justifying inclusion or exclusion, is a
Statement of Applicability exercise (`iso-27001-clause-6-1-3-d-soa`) performed
against verified facts about a specific system. This corpus supplies the
identifiers; it never supplies the selection.

## 5. Relationship to the GDPR — resolved to a **verified absence**, 2026-07-31

### `iso-gdpr-art-42-relationship`

- **instrument:** — (this entry records that a regulator statement **does not
  exist**) — *standard / regulator-decision boundary question*
- **provision:** whether ISO/IEC 27001 is a GDPR Art. 42 / Art. 43 certification
  mechanism
- **text_form:** `topic only`
- **text:** **No EDPB guideline, opinion or approved-certification record was
  found stating that ISO/IEC 27001 is, or is treated as, a GDPR Art. 42 / Art. 43
  certification mechanism or European Data Protection Seal.** What the EDPB has
  actually done under Art. 42(5) / 70(1)(o) is approve **distinct, GDPR-native
  certification criteria** as European Data Protection Seals — the clearest
  located example being **"Europrivacy"**, first approved by EDPB Opinion 28/2022
  (10 October 2022), with a further EDPB opinion in the 2026 series recognising
  the Europrivacy criteria as an Art. 46 transfer tool as well. ISO/IEC 27001
  appears in EDPB certification materials only as one **input standard** a
  scheme's criteria may reference or build on — **not** as a GDPR certification
  mechanism in its own right. Multiple independent commentary sources
  (ISMS.online, Advisera, IAPP) say the same. ISO 27001 certification can serve
  as supporting evidence of diligence on security measures (relevant to

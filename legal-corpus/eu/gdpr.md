## Information duties and data-subject rights

### `gdpr-art-13`

- **instrument:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation)

### `gdpr-art-20`

- **instrument:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation)
- **provision:** Article 20 — "Right to data portability" / "Diritto alla
  portabilità dei dati"
- **text_form:** **verbatim** (EN and IT, all four paragraphs)
- **text (EN, para. 1 — read the gate carefully):** the data subject has the
  right to receive the personal data **which he or she has provided to a
  controller**, "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format",
  and to transmit them to another controller without hindrance, **where:**
  "(a) the processing is based on consent pursuant to point (a) of Article 6(1)
  or point (a) of Article 9(2) **or on a contract pursuant to point (b) of
  Article 6(1)**; **and** (b) the processing is carried out by **automated
  means**." Para. 2 adds direct controller-to-controller transmission "where
  technically feasible". Para. 3: the right "shall be without prejudice to
  Article 17" and does not apply to public-interest/official-authority
  processing. Para. 4: it must not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of
  others.
- **official_url:**
  https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/IT/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679 ·
  official Italian OJ PDF: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/ita/pdf
- **consulted:** direct `curl` through the `r.jina.ai` proxy against the full
  EUR-Lex GDPR document — **verification_grade:** `proxy-sourced`
- **language_version:** English **and Italian**, both verbatim
- **verified:** 2026-07-31
- **legal_status:** `in force`
- **notes — THE ASYMMETRY, and any framing that collapses it is wrong.**
  **Art. 20 is basis-gated; Art. 15 is not.** Three concrete differences:
  1. **Art. 15 applies regardless of lawful basis. Art. 20 applies only where
     the basis is consent or contract, *and* processing is automated.**
  2. **The output standard is stricter.** Art. 20 requires "structured, commonly
     used **and machine-readable**"; Art. 15(3) requires only "a commonly used
     electronic form". A PDF or a printed pack can satisfy Art. 15 and fail
     Art. 20.
  3. **The scope is narrower.** Art. 20 reaches only data the data subject
     *provided*; Art. 15 reaches all personal data being processed.

   **"One manual procedure satisfies both rights identically" is wrong on the
   statute.** It may satisfy Art. 15 while failing Art. 20's format requirement,
   and it may be unnecessary for Art. 20 entirely if no consent/contract basis
   applies.

### `gdpr-art-18`

- **provision:** Article 18 — "Right to restriction of processing"
- **text_form:** **verbatim** (all three paragraphs)
- **text (EN):** "1. The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the
  controller restriction of processing where one of the following applies:
  (a) the accuracy of the personal data is contested by the data subject, for a
  period enabling the controller to verify the accuracy of the personal data;
  (b) the processing is unlawful and the data subject opposes the erasure of the
  personal data and requests the restriction of their use instead; (c) the
  controller no longer needs the personal data for the purposes of the
  processing, but they are required by the data subject for the establishment,
  exercise or defence of legal claims; (d) the data subject has objected to
  processing pursuant to Article 21(1) pending the verification whether the
  legitimate grounds of the controller override those of the data subject.
  2. Where processing has been restricted under paragraph 1, such personal data
  shall, with the exception of storage, only be processed with the data
  subject's consent or for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal
  claims or for the protection of the rights of another natural or legal person
  or for reasons of important public interest of the Union or of a Member State.
  3. A data subject who has obtained restriction of processing pursuant to
  paragraph 1 shall be informed by the controller before the restriction of
  processing is lifted."
- **verified:** 2026-08-05 — transcribed from the retained 2026-08-05 EUR-Lex EN
  snapshot, `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** A **middle state between full processing and erasure**: the data
  are kept (storage is always allowed) but their further processing is limited
  to the listed grounds. Point (d) makes restriction the **automatic pendant of

  `gdpr-art-21-1`. Lifting the restriction requires **prior notice to the data
  subject** (para. 3). Triggers the `gdpr-art-19` notification duty.

### `gdpr-art-21`

- **provision:** Article 21 — "Right to object" (all six paragraphs)
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "1. The data subject shall have the right to object, on grounds
  relating to his or her particular situation, at any time to processing of
  personal data concerning him or her which is based on point (e) or (f) of
  Article 6(1), including profiling based on those provisions. The controller
  shall no longer process the personal data unless the controller demonstrates
  compelling legitimate grounds for the processing which override the
  interests, rights and freedoms of the data subject or for the establishment,
  exercise or defence of legal claims. 2. Where personal data are processed for
  direct marketing purposes, the data subject shall have the right to object at
  any time to processing of personal data concerning him or her for such
  marketing, which includes profiling to the extent that it is related to such
  direct marketing. 3. Where the data subject objects to processing for direct
  marketing purposes, the personal data shall no longer be processed for such
  purposes. 4. At the latest at the time of the first communication with the
  data subject, the right referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 shall be explicitly
  brought to the attention of the data subject and shall be presented clearly
  and separately from any other information. 5. In the context of the use of
  information society services, and notwithstanding Directive 2002/58/EC, the
  data subject may exercise his or her right to object by automated means using
  technical specifications. 6. Where personal data are processed for scientific
  or historical research purposes or statistical purposes pursuant to Article
  89(1), the data subject, on grounds relating to his or her particular
  situation, shall have the right to object to processing of personal data
  concerning him or her, unless the processing is necessary for the performance
  of a task carried out for reasons of public interest."
- **verified:** 2026-08-05 — transcribed from the retained 2026-08-05 EUR-Lex EN
  snapshot, `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** **The counterweight to `gdpr-art-6-1-f`** (legitimate interests):
  objection under para. 1 shifts the burden to the controller to demonstrate
  **compelling** legitimate grounds. Para. 2's direct-marketing objection is
  **absolute and basis-independent** (paras. 2 and 3), and para. 4 imposes a
  **standalone transparency duty** — the objection right must be brought to the
  data subject's attention "clearly and separately from any other information"
  at first communication, i.e. alongside the Art. 13/14 notice. An objection
  under para. 1 is one of the six erasure grounds of `gdpr-art-17-1(c)` and

## Erasure

### `gdpr-art-17-1`

- **provision:** Article 17(1) — right to erasure ("right to be forgotten")
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (all **six** grounds, (a)–(f))
- **text:** The data subject has the right to obtain erasure without undue delay
  where one of six grounds applies: (a) the data are no longer necessary for the
  purposes; (b) consent is withdrawn and there is no other basis; (c) the data
  subject objects under Art. 21 and there are no overriding legitimate grounds;
  (d) the data have been unlawfully processed; (e) erasure is required by a legal
  obligation; **(f) the data were collected in relation to the offer of
  information society services referred to in Article 8(1)**.
- **notes:** Art. 17(1) has **six** grounds, (a)–(f), not four. Ground (f) —
  data collected in relation to the offer of information society services under
  Art. 8(1) — is easy to omit, and any "the grounds are…" statement that lists
  fewer than six is incomplete. Verify against this entry, not against memory
  of it.

## By design and by default

### `gdpr-art-25-1`

- **provision:** Article 25(1) — data protection by design
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the

### `gdpr-art-25-2`

- **provision:** Article 25(2) — data protection by default
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (Group A)
- **text:** By default, only personal data necessary for each specific purpose
  may be processed — as to the amount collected, the extent of processing, the

  indefinite number of natural persons.

### `gdpr-art-28-1`

- **provision:** Article 28(1)
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (Group A)
- **text:** Where processing is carried out on a controller's behalf, the
  controller must use only processors providing sufficient guarantees to
  implement appropriate technical and organisational measures meeting the
  Regulation's requirements and ensuring protection of data-subject rights.

### `gdpr-art-28-3-a`

- **provision:** Article 28(3)(a) — the processor acts only on documented
  instructions from the controller
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "(a) processes the personal data only on documented instructions
  from the controller, including with regard to transfers of personal data to a
  third country or an international organisation, unless required to do so by
  Union or Member State law to which the processor is subject; in such a case,
  the processor shall inform the controller of that legal requirement before
  processing, unless that law prohibits such information on important grounds of
  public interest;"
- **notes:** This governs the **controller-to-processor** relationship: what an
  *external* processor may do with its controller's instructions. It is **not**
  the anchor for internal staff acting without proper authorization inside the
  controller's own organisation. That is **`gdpr-art-32-4`** ("any natural
  person acting under the authority of the controller… does not process it except
  on instructions from the controller"), which is the correct citation for that
  situation. The EDPB's own gloss is at
  `edpb-guidelines-07-2020.md` → `edpb-gl-07-2020-part-ii-1-3-1-4`.

### `gdpr-art-28-3-b`

- **provision:** Article 28(3)(b) — processor staff confidentiality
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "(b) ensures that persons authorised to process the personal
  data have committed themselves to confidentiality or are under an appropriate
  statutory obligation of confidentiality;"
- **verified:** 2026-08-05 — the 2026-07-31 snapshot was cleared; re-fetched
  through the `r.jina.ai` proxy, still `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** The textual anchor for a processor-side confidentiality duty on
  staff with access to personal data. Note the disjunction: the commitment may
  be **contractual or statutory** — the provision does not itself require a
  written NDA, and it binds the *processor's* staff, not the controller's
  (for the controller's own staff see `gdpr-art-32-4`).

### `gdpr-art-28-3-c`

- **provision:** Article 28(3)(c) — security measures pursuant to Article 32
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "(c) takes all measures required pursuant to Article 32;"

  through the `r.jina.ai` proxy, still `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** The processor-side flow-down of the **whole** of Art. 32 — "all
  measures required pursuant to Article 32" is unqualified. It reads together
  with the controller-side security anchors in this corpus
  (`gdpr-art-32-1-b` — CIA plus resilience — and `gdpr-art-32-4` — processing
  only on instructions) and with the processor's assistance duty in
  `gdpr-art-28-3-f`. This is the clause a DPA's "security" section implements;

### `gdpr-art-28-3-d`

- **provision:** Article 28(3)(d) — engaging another processor
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "(d) respects the conditions referred to in paragraphs 2 and 4

### `gdpr-art-28-3-e`

- **provision:** Article 28(3)(e) — assisting the controller with data-subject
  rights
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "(e) taking into account the nature of the processing, assists
  the controller by appropriate technical and organisational measures, insofar
  as this is possible, for the fulfilment of the controller's obligation to

  Chapter III;"
- **verified:** 2026-08-05 — the 2026-07-31 snapshot was cleared; re-fetched

- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** The processor-side mirror of the Chapter III rights the corpus
  already carries as controller obligations — `gdpr-art-15` (access),
  `gdpr-art-17-1` (erasure), `gdpr-art-20` (portability). The processor's duty
  is **assistance, bounded by what is possible** ("insofar as this is
  possible") and by the nature of the processing — it is not a duty to answer
  data-subject requests on the controller's behalf.

### `gdpr-art-28-3-f`

- **provision:** Article 28(3)(f) — assistance with the controller's compliance
  obligations, Articles 32 to 36
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "(f) assists the controller in ensuring compliance with the
  obligations pursuant to Articles 32 to 36 taking into account the nature of
  processing and the information available to the processor;"
- **verified:** 2026-08-05 — the 2026-07-31 snapshot was cleared; re-fetched
  through the `r.jina.ai` proxy, still `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** The processor's assistance duty spans **Arts. 32–36**: security
  (32), breach notification to the authority (33), breach communication to data
  subjects (34), DPIA (35) and **prior consultation (36)** — every one of which
  this corpus carries as a controller obligation (`gdpr-art-32-1-b`,
  `gdpr-art-32-4`, `gdpr-art-33-1`, `gdpr-art-33-3`, `gdpr-art-33-5`,
  `gdpr-art-34`, `gdpr-art-35-1-3`, `gdpr-art-35-4`, `gdpr-art-36`). The duty
  is bounded twice — "taking into account the nature of processing" and "the
  information available to the processor" — the processor **assists** in
  ensuring compliance, it does not guarantee it. This is the textual hook for
  the DPA's breach/DPIA/consultation assistance clauses.

### `gdpr-art-28-3-g`

- **provision:** Article 28(3)(g) — return or deletion at the end of services
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "(g) at the choice of the controller, deletes or returns all
  the personal data to the controller after the end of the provision of
  services relating to processing, and deletes existing copies unless Union or
  Member State law requires storage of the personal data;"
- **verified:** 2026-08-05 — the 2026-07-31 snapshot was cleared; re-fetched
  through the `r.jina.ai` proxy, still `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** The return/deletion point: the **controller** chooses delete or
  return; either way the processor must delete existing copies unless Union or
  Member State law requires storage. Two sharp edges. **(1) There is no
  Art. 28(3)(i).** The first subparagraph of Art. 28(3) has points (a)–(h)
  only — verified against the full fetched text 2026-08-05; the return/deletion
  obligation **is point (g)**, and a citation of "28(3)(i)" would be a
  fabrication. **(2) The "unless … law requires storage" carve-out** is the
  textual hook for a processor's lawful retention after service ends (e.g.
  Italian accounting-retention duties — `it-cc-art-2220` and the DPR-based
  duties in `../national/it-accounting-retention.md`); it excuses storage, not processing,
  and what each instrument requires is a determination for the controller, not this
  entry's.

### `gdpr-art-28-3-h`

- **provision:** Article 28(3)(h) — minimum contract content
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "(h) makes available to the controller all information necessary
  to demonstrate compliance with the obligations laid down in this Article and
  allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, conducted by the
  controller or another auditor mandated by the controller."
- **notes:** Point (h) is an **audit right the controller holds against its
  processor**, exercised through the DPA. It **cannot** be authority for an
  *internal* audit-trail or access-logging gap inside the controller's own
  systems — it says nothing about what a controller must log about its own
  staff. **The correct anchors for an internal-logging finding are
  `gdpr-art-5-1-f`, `gdpr-art-24` and `gdpr-art-32-1-b`; for the
  breach-reconstruction capability specifically, `gdpr-art-33-3`.**

## Records, security, breach

### `gdpr-art-30-1`

- **provision:** Article 30(1) and 30(2) — records of processing activities
  (controller side and processor side)
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (Group A)
- **text:** Each controller (and its representative, where applicable) must
  maintain a record of processing activities under its responsibility,
  containing controller/DPO contact details, purposes, categories of data
  subjects and of personal data, categories of recipients (including in third
  countries), international transfers and their safeguards, envisaged erasure
  time limits where possible, and a general description of the Article 32(1)
  security measures where possible. Paragraph 2 sets the parallel, shorter
  record a **processor** must keep.

### `gdpr-art-30-5`

- **provision:** Article 30(5) — the fewer-than-250-employees carve-out
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "The obligations referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 shall not
  apply to an enterprise or organisation employing fewer than 250 persons
  **unless** the processing it carries out is likely to result in a risk to the
  rights and freedoms of data subjects, the processing is not occasional, or the
  processing includes special categories of data…"
- **notes:** **New in 2026-07-31 — this threshold was not in the corpus at all**,
  and it is a material scoping fact for a smaller controller or processor. Read
  the three "unless" limbs carefully before relying on it: **routine, non-
  occasional business processing defeats the carve-out**, which is how most
  controllers under 250 employees still owe the record. Do **not** confuse this
  250-employee figure with the NIS2 size gate's 250-employee figure
  (`eu-scope-definitions.md`) — different instruments, different tests, same
  number, and conflating them is an easy and invisible error.

### `gdpr-art-32-1-b`

- **provision:** Article 32(1)(b) — security of processing
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (Group A; all four paragraphs were fetched, and
  paragraph 1's (a)–(d) breakdown — pseudonymisation/encryption; CIA plus
  resilience; restoration timeliness; regular testing — is individually
  addressable in the retained snapshot if a future citation needs one sub-point)
- **text:** Controller and processor must ensure, as appropriate, the ongoing
  confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience of processing systems
  and services.

### `gdpr-art-32-4`

- **provision:** Article 32(4)
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (Group A)
- **text:** Controller and processor must ensure that any natural person acting
  under their authority who has access to personal data does not process it
  except on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union
  or Member State law.
- **notes:** **This — not Art. 28(3)(a) — is the anchor for an
  internal-authorization finding.** See `gdpr-art-28-3-a`.

### `gdpr-art-33-1`

- **provision:** Article 33(1) — notification of a breach to the supervisory
  authority
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (Group A; all five paragraphs fetched)
- **text:** On becoming aware of a personal data breach, the controller must
  notify the competent supervisory authority without undue delay and, where
  feasible, not later than 72 hours after becoming aware of it, unless the
  breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural
  persons; a notification made later than 72 hours must be accompanied by the
  reasons for the delay.
- **notes:** A common mis-citation, worth stating plainly: a requirement that a breach be
  "**reconstructable** within 72 hours" is **not** textual. It is a practical
  consequence of this duty and must be written as an inference, never quoted as
  if Article 33 said it. **The textual hook for that capability is 33(3)(a)** —
  see the next entry.

### `gdpr-art-33-3`

- **provision:** Article 33(3) — what a breach notification must contain
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "3. The notification referred to in paragraph 1 shall at least:
  (a) describe the nature of the personal data breach including where possible,
  **the categories and approximate number of data subjects concerned** and the
  categories and approximate number of personal data records concerned;
  (b) communicate the name and contact details of the data protection officer or
  other contact point where more information can be obtained; (c) describe the
  likely consequences of the personal data breach; (d) describe the measures
  taken or proposed to be taken by the controller to address the personal data
  breach, including, where appropriate, measures to mitigate its possible adverse
  effects."
- **notes:** **Point (a) is the correct, narrow, defensible citation for what an
  absent internal audit trail actually costs.** A controller that cannot
  determine "the categories and approximate number of data subjects concerned"
  because it lacks the logging to reconstruct who accessed what has a **concrete
  Art. 33(3)(a) capability gap** — not a free-floating "good practice"
  observation, and not an Art. 28(3) issue (a different relationship entirely).

### `gdpr-art-33-5`

- **provision:** Article 33(5) — the internal breach register
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "5. The controller shall document any personal data breaches,
  comprising the facts relating to the personal data breach, its effects and the
  remedial action taken. That documentation shall enable the supervisory
  authority to verify compliance with this Article."
- **notes:** This duty applies to **every** breach, including those not notified
  because they were unlikely to result in a risk. "We decided not to notify" is
  itself a record this paragraph requires.

### `gdpr-art-34`

- **provision:** Article 34 — "Communication of a personal data breach to the
  data subject"
- **text_form:** **verbatim** (all four paragraphs)
- **text (EN):** "1. When the personal data breach is likely to result in a high
  risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller shall
  communicate the personal data breach to the data subject without undue delay.
  2. The communication to the data subject referred to in paragraph 1 of this
  Article shall describe in clear and plain language the nature of the personal
  data breach and contain at least the information and measures referred to in
  points (b), (c) and (d) of Article 33(3). 3. The communication to the data
  subject referred to in paragraph 1 shall not be required if any of the
  following conditions are met: (a) the controller has implemented appropriate
  technical and organisational protection measures, and those measures were
  applied to the personal data affected by the personal data breach, in
  particular those that render the personal data unintelligible to any person
  who is not authorised to access it, such as encryption; (b) the controller has
  taken subsequent measures which ensure that the high risk to the rights and
  freedoms of data subjects referred to in paragraph 1 is no longer likely to
  materialise; (c) it would involve disproportionate effort. In such a case,
  there shall instead be a public communication or similar measure whereby the
  data subjects are informed in an equally effective manner. 4. If the
  controller has not already communicated the personal data breach to the data
  subject, the supervisory authority, having considered the likelihood of the
  personal data breach resulting in a high risk, may require it to do so or may
  decide that any of the conditions referred to in paragraph 3 are met."
- **verified:** 2026-08-05 — the 2026-07-31 snapshot was cleared; re-fetched
  through the `r.jina.ai` proxy, `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** The **third limb of the breach triad** with `gdpr-art-33-1` (to
  the authority, 72 hours) and `gdpr-art-33-3` (content). Art. 34 gates
  **data-subject** notification on a **high-risk** threshold — higher than
  Art. 33(1)'s "likely to result in a risk" — and Art. 34(3) carries three
  exemptions: encryption or equivalent rendering unintelligible, subsequent
  measures that neutralise the high risk, and disproportionate effort (with a
  public-communication substitute). **The keyed asymmetry to remember:** a
  breach that clears the Art. 33(1) bar but not the Art. 34(1) bar is notified
  to the authority but **not** to data subjects. Whether any given breach meets
  the high-risk threshold is a fact-specific determination for the controller;
  this entry owns the statute only.

### `gdpr-art-35-1-3`

- **provision:** Article 35(1) and 35(3) — data protection impact assessment
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (Group A; all eleven paragraphs fetched)
- **text:** A DPIA is required before processing that is likely to result in a
  high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, in particular where
  new technologies are used, having regard to the nature, scope, context and
  purposes of the processing. Paragraph 3 lists non-exhaustive mandatory cases:
  (a) systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects based on automated
  processing, including profiling, producing legal or similarly significant
  effects; (b) large-scale processing of Article 9 special-category or
  Article 10 criminal-conviction data; (c) systematic large-scale monitoring of
  a publicly accessible area.
- **notes:** Art. 35(3)'s three listed cases are **non-exhaustive**, so failing
  to match any of them is not by itself a finding that no DPIA is required —
  the general Art. 35(1) high-risk test still applies. A supervisory
  authority's own national list (the Art. 35(4) basis, next entry) adds further
  mandatory cases: **read the applicable national list before concluding "no
  DPIA needed"** — one retrieved example is carried at
  `../case-law/index.md` → `garante-elenco-dpia-2018`.

### `gdpr-art-35-4`

- **provision:** Article 35(4) — the supervisory authority's own DPIA list
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "The supervisory authority shall establish and make public a
  list of the kind of processing operations which are subject to the requirement
  for a data protection impact assessment…"
- **notes:** **This is the legal basis for the Garante's list**
  (`garante-elenco-dpia-2018`). A national list supplements Art. 35(3); it does

### `gdpr-art-36`

- **provision:** Article 36 — "Prior consultation" with the supervisory
  authority
- **text_form:** **verbatim** (all five paragraphs)
- **text (EN):** "1. The controller shall consult the supervisory authority
  prior to processing where a data protection impact assessment under Article
  35 indicates that the processing would result in a high risk in the absence
  of measures taken by the controller to mitigate the risk. 2. Where the
  supervisory authority is of the opinion that the intended processing referred
  to in paragraph 1 would infringe this Regulation, in particular where the
  controller has insufficiently identified or mitigated the risk, the
  supervisory authority shall, within period of up to eight weeks of receipt of
  the request for consultation, provide written advice to the controller and,
  where applicable to the processor, and may use any of its powers referred to
  in Article 58. That period may be extended by six weeks, taking into account
  the complexity of the intended processing. The supervisory authority shall
  inform the controller and, where applicable, the processor, of any such
  extension within one month of receipt of the request for consultation
  together with the reasons for the delay. Those periods may be suspended until
  the supervisory authority has obtained information it has requested for the
  purposes of the consultation. 3. When consulting the supervisory authority
  pursuant to paragraph 1, the controller shall provide the supervisory
  authority with: (a) where applicable, the respective responsibilities of the
  controller, joint controllers and processors involved in the processing, in
  particular for processing within a group of undertakings; (b) the purposes
  and means of the intended processing; (c) the measures and safeguards
  provided to protect the rights and freedoms of data subjects pursuant to this
  Regulation; (d) where applicable, the contact details of the data protection
  officer; (e) the data protection impact assessment provided for in Article
  35; and (f) any other information requested by the supervisory authority. 4.

- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** The **prior-consultation** obligation: it is triggered where a

### `gdpr-art-45`

- **provision:** Article 45 — "Transfers on the basis of an adequacy decision"

- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN, paras. 1 and 3 — the operative structure):** "1. A transfer of

  transfer shall not require any specific authorisation. … 3. The Commission,
  after assessing the adequacy of the level of protection, may decide, by means

  ensures an adequate level of protection within the meaning of paragraph 2 of
  this Article. The implementing act shall provide for a mechanism for a
  periodic review, at least every four years, which shall take into account all
  relevant developments in the third country or international organisation. The
  implementing act shall specify its territorial and sectoral application and,
  where applicable, identify the supervisory authority or authorities referred
  to in point (b) of paragraph 2 of this Article. The implementing act shall be
  adopted in accordance with the examination procedure referred to in Article
  93(2)."
- **text (EN, the remaining paragraphs, structure only):** para. 2 lists the
  three assessment elements (rule of law and redress (a), an independent
  supervisory authority with enforcement powers (b), international commitments
  (c)); para. 4 puts the Commission under an ongoing monitoring duty; para. 5
  empowers repeal/amendment/suspension where adequacy is lost, with **no
  retro-active effect**; para. 6 requires consultations; para. 7 preserves

  snapshot, `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** **The adequacy basis, now on primary text.** Para. 2(b) — "one or
  more independent supervisory authorities" in the recipient country — is the
  provision the EU–US DPF litigation turns on (`eu-us-dpf-adequacy.md`,
  `in force — under appeal`). Para. 7 is the practical safety net: loss of
  adequacy does **not** retro-actively invalidate transfers, which then rest on
  Arts. 46–49. Para. 8's published list is where the EU–US DPF decision
  (`eu-dpf-adequacy-2023-1795`) sits. The Commission's SCC decision
  (`scc-2021-914`) is the Art. 46(2)(c) sibling of this article — see
  `scc-2021-914.md`.

### `gdpr-art-46-1-2c`

- **provision:** Article 46(1) and 46(2)(c) — appropriate safeguards
- **text_form:** **normalized summary** — a faithful restatement, **not the law's words; do not quote it**. Provenance of the fetch (Group A; no new substance, provenance only)
- **text:** In the absence of an adequacy decision, a transfer may take place
  only if appropriate safeguards are provided and enforceable data-subject
  rights and effective legal remedies are available; standard contractual
  clauses adopted by the Commission are one of the listed safeguards.
- **notes:** The common ordering for a third-country processor is adequacy
  first, standard contractual clauses as the fallback where no adequacy
  decision covers the transfer. The clause set itself is carried at
  `scc-2021-914.md`; an adequacy decision and its contested status at
  `eu-us-dpf-adequacy.md`.

### `gdpr-art-77`

- **provision:** Article 77 — "Right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory

- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "1. Without prejudice to any other administrative or judicial

  data subject considers that the processing of personal data relating to him

### `gdpr-art-82`

- **provision:** Article 82 — "Right to compensation and liability" (all six
  paragraphs)
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "1. Any person who has suffered material or non-material damage
  as a result of an infringement of this Regulation shall have the right to
  receive compensation from the controller or processor for the damage
  suffered. 2. Any controller involved in processing shall be liable for the
  damage caused by processing which infringes this Regulation. A processor
  shall be liable for the damage caused by processing only where it has not
  complied with obligations of this Regulation specifically directed to
  processors or where it has acted outside or contrary to lawful instructions
  of the controller. 3. A controller or processor shall be exempt from
  liability under paragraph 2 if it proves that it is not in any way
  responsible for the event giving rise to the damage. 4. Where more than one
  controller or processor, or both a controller and a processor, are involved
  in the same processing and where they are, under paragraphs 2 and 3,
  responsible for any damage caused by processing, each controller or processor
  shall be held liable for the entire damage in order to ensure effective
  compensation of the data subject. 5. Where a controller or processor has, in
  accordance with paragraph 4, paid full compensation for the damage suffered,
  that controller or processor shall be entitled to claim back from the other
  controllers or processors involved in the same processing that part of the
  compensation corresponding to their part of responsibility for the damage, in
  accordance with the conditions set out in paragraph 2. 6. Court proceedings
  for exercising the right to receive compensation shall be brought before the
  courts competent under the law of the Member State referred to in Article
  79(2)."
- **verified:** 2026-08-05 — transcribed from the retained 2026-08-05 EUR-Lex EN
  snapshot, `proxy-sourced`.
- **language_version:** English only (IT not captured in this pass)
- **notes:** **The compensation right**: material **or non-material** damage
  (non-material includes the distress/control-loss class of damage the CJEU has
  held recoverable), against controller or processor. Para. 2 draws the
  **controller/processor liability split** — a processor is liable only for
  its own breaches or acting outside/contrary to the controller's lawful
  instructions (the mirror of `gdpr-art-28-3-a` and `gdpr-art-32-4`). Para. 3
  carries a **defence**: proof of non-responsibility. Para. 4 makes liability
  **joint and several toward the data subject** (each responsible party liable
  for the entire damage), with para. 5's **contribution claims** between the
  parties — the statutory basis for the cross-liability and indemnity clauses
  found in a data processing agreement. Para. 6 routes the forum through
  Art. 79(2).

**Why this page exists.** Two other pages lean on this instrument without
holding it. The Cyber Resilience Act's sector exclusion names it
(`cra-art-2-2-4` in `cra.md`), so whether a product with digital elements falls
under the CRA at all can turn on whether it is a medical device. And the GDPR's
special-category derogation in Art. 9(2)(i) (`gdpr-art-9` in `gdpr.md`) speaks
of the quality and safety of medical devices. A consumer who reaches either
needs the definition and the scope article, read from the publisher, and needs
to know which edition binds today.

### The amendment trap is live on this instrument

The original and the consolidated text disagree on dates that a reader is most
likely to quote. The act as adopted says, at Art. 123(2), "It shall apply from
26 May 2020."; the consolidated text says 26 May 2021, the date Regulation (EU)
2020/561 substituted. Fetch only the original and the wrong date reads as
settled law. The same amending act moved the Art. 1(2) dates, and the Eudamed
clock in Art. 123(3)(d) was rewritten by Regulation (EU) 2024/1860. The
transitional regime in Art. 120 carries `M1`, `M3` and `M5` markers. Cite this
instrument from the consolidated text, with its as-at date.

**Two amending acts sit outside the consolidated header.** The Publications
Office index, queried on 2026-10-14, records ten acts that amend
`32017R0745`: the eight above, plus Commission Delegated Regulations (EU)
2023/2197 and (EU) 2025/1920. Both were fetched from CELLAR in the same pass and
read: each amends the Regulation "as regards the assignment of Unique Device
Identifiers" (for contact lenses, and for spectacle frames, spectacle lenses
and ready-to-wear reading spectacles respectively). Neither touches a
provision recorded on this page. Why the consolidation does not list them was
not established; a pass that needs the UDI rules should read both acts
directly rather than rely on the consolidated text alone. The index listed no
consolidation later than 19 July 2026.

## Scope and definitions

### `mdr-art-1-1`

- **provision:** Article 1(1), "Subject matter and scope"
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "This Regulation lays down rules concerning the placing on the
  market, making available on the market or putting into service of medical
  devices for human use and accessories for such devices in the Union. This
  Regulation also applies to clinical investigations concerning such medical
  devices and accessories conducted in the Union."
- **legal_status:** `in force` · **applies_from:** 2021-05-26 (Art. 123(2), as
  amended — see `mdr-art-123-1-2`)
- **notes:** paragraph 1 reads the same in both editions. Art. 1 goes on to
  extend the Regulation to the Annex XVI products without a medical purpose
  (para. 2, dates amended by 2020/561), and para. 6 lists what it does not
  cover, starting with in vitro diagnostic devices under Regulation (EU)
  2017/746, which has no page in this corpus. Those paragraphs were read in this
  pass and are **not recorded** here, so they are not citable from this corpus.

### `mdr-art-2-1`

- **provision:** Article 2, point (1) — "medical device"
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "'medical device' means any instrument, apparatus, appliance,
  software, implant, reagent, material or other article intended by the
  manufacturer to be used, alone or in combination, for human beings for one or
  more of the following specific medical purposes: — diagnosis, prevention,
  monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease, —
  diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of, or compensation for, an
  injury or disability, — investigation, replacement or modification of the
  anatomy or of a physiological or pathological process or state, — providing
  information by means of in vitro examination of specimens derived from the
  human body, including organ, blood and tissue donations, and which does not
  achieve its principal intended action by pharmacological, immunological or
  metabolic means, in or on the human body, but which may be assisted in its
  function by such means. The following products shall also be deemed to be
  medical devices: — devices for the control or support of conception; —
  products specifically intended for the cleaning, disinfection or sterilisation
  of devices as referred to in Article 1(4) and of those referred to in the
  first paragraph of this point."
- **legal_status:** `in force` · **applies_from:** 2021-05-26
- **notes:** identical in both editions; no amendment or corrigendum marker
  falls inside point (1). **Software is named in the definition itself**, and
  the test turns on what the manufacturer **intends** (`mdr-art-2-12`), not on
  what the product could be used for. That is the reason this corpus cannot
  answer whether a given piece of software is a medical device: the answer
  depends on the manufacturer's stated purpose, which is a fact about a
  product, not about the law. The classification of software that is a device
  (Annex VIII) is not recorded here.

### `mdr-art-2-2`

- **provision:** Article 2, point (2) — "accessory for a medical device"
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "'accessory for a medical device' means an article which,
  whilst not being itself a medical device, is intended by its manufacturer to
  be used together with one or several particular medical device(s) to
  specifically enable the medical device(s) to be used in accordance with
  its/their intended purpose(s) or to specifically and directly assist the
  medical functionality of the medical device(s) in terms of its/their intended
  purpose(s);"
- **legal_status:** `in force` · **applies_from:** 2021-05-26
- **notes:** identical in both editions. An accessory is in scope by
  `mdr-art-1-1` without being a medical device itself, which matters to any
  reading of a sector exclusion that names this Regulation.

### `mdr-art-2-12`

- **provision:** Article 2, point (12) — "intended purpose"
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN):** "'intended purpose' means the use for which a device is
  intended according to the data supplied by the manufacturer on the label, in
  the instructions for use or in promotional or sales materials or statements
  and as specified by the manufacturer in the clinical evaluation;"
- **legal_status:** `in force` · **applies_from:** 2021-05-26
- **notes:** identical in both editions. Promotional and sales material counts
  toward the intended purpose alongside the label and the instructions for use.

## Dates

### `mdr-art-123-1-2`

- **provision:** Article 123(1) and (2), "Entry into force and date of
  application"
- **text_form:** **verbatim**
- **text (EN, consolidated as at 19 July 2026):** "1. This Regulation shall
  enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the
  Official Journal of the European Union. 2. It shall apply from 26 May 2021."
- **text (EN, act as adopted — superseded on this point, kept for the trap):**
  "2. It shall apply from 26 May 2020."
- **legal_status:** `in force` · **applies_from:** 2021-05-26 for the general
  limb. Entry into force was 25 May 2017, the twentieth day after publication in
  OJ L 117 of 5 May 2017; the CELLAR metadata lists 2017-05-25 among the act's
  dates.
- **notes:** **The date most likely to be mis-cited.** `26 May 2021` is the
  consolidated text, substituted by Regulation (EU) 2020/561 (`M1` marker in the
  consolidated text); the original's `26 May 2020` no longer binds. Both figures
  are primary-fetched. Art. 123(3) carries derogations with their own clocks,
  read in this pass and **not recorded** here, among them obligations tied to
  the European database on medical devices that apply from a date counted
  **six months from the publication of a Commission notice** (Art. 123(3)(d), as
  rewritten by Regulation (EU) 2024/1860). That date is a formula per system,
  never a calendar date, and must not be written as one. Devices still placed on
  the market under the transitional regime of Art. 120 (amended by 2020/561,
  2023/607 and 2024/1860) are a separate question that this page does not
  record.

## Software and IT security

### `mdr-annex-i-17`

- **provision:** Annex I, Chapter II, Section 17, "Electronic programmable
  systems — devices that incorporate electronic programmable systems and
  software that are devices in themselves" — points 17.2 and 17.4
- **text_form:** **verbatim** (17.2 and 17.4; 17.1 and 17.3 summarized in notes)
- **text (EN, 17.2):** "For devices that incorporate software or for software
  that are devices in themselves, the software shall be developed and
  manufactured in accordance with the state of the art taking into account the
  principles of development life cycle, risk management, including information
  security, verification and validation."
- **text (EN, 17.4):** "Manufacturers shall set out minimum requirements
  concerning hardware, IT networks characteristics and IT security measures,
  including protection against unauthorised access, necessary to run the
  software as intended."
- **legal_status:** `in force` · **applies_from:** 2021-05-26
- **notes:** Section 17 reads the same in both editions; no amendment marker
  falls inside it. These two points are the Regulation's own security
  requirement for device software, and they are the reason a product excluded
  from the CRA by `cra-art-2-2-4` is not thereby free of security duties. The
  two regimes' requirements are not the same text and must not be read across:
  cite each from its own entry. 17.1 (repeatability, reliability, performance;
  single-fault risk) and 17.3 (mobile-platform design) were read and are
  summarized here only; they are not quotable from this page.
